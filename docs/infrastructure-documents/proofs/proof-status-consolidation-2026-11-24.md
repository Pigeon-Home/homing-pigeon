## 2. What remains genuinely open

1. The propagation constrained non Horn optimum of row 44, settled under the definitions named in that row. At width three, under ordinary unit propagation, the least auxiliary count for an exact encoding of R_m that is visibly propagation complete is max(0, m − 2), and the same count holds with full propagation completeness in place of the visible form; at m = 256 both counts are 254. The settling argument is the relayed addendum argument of 27 November 2026; its arithmetic is checked, and no project test pins the general statement, so the settled status belongs to the definitions named in row 44 and to no other reading, and under any different definition the question stays open. The 13 by 20 grid of row 15 shows what a smaller count costs in propagation strength, and the Horn chain shows a fully propagation complete encoding at 254 auxiliaries for m = 256.
2. The priority of the gluing lemma of rows 26 and 45. The neighbouring results are located and named on the fixed width page and in the two reviews that searched for it, and the one shared variable form was not located in the sources searched. Whether some source outside that search states it remains unsettled, so the lemma stands as a relayed candidate argument with strong finite evidence beside it.
3. The general forms behind several verified finite rows. The capacity equivalence of row 6 at general k, the hierarchy tradeoff of row 13 as an asymptotic statement, and the gluing lemma of row 26 are arguments whose finite content the project has pinned and whose general content the project has not independently reproved. The table keeps those two layers separate on purpose.
4. Candidate C on the real machine, per row 46. The finite map is a control instrument at finite size. It killed and cleared named pairs for its own small machine, and it transfers nothing by itself to the real universal interpreter.
5. The relayed batches of rows 27 to 43 as a body. They arrived faster than the review machinery ran. Each one awaits either a project review of the same kind as rounds one to three, or a deliberate decision to leave it as a relayed argument in ComChat only. Until one of those happens, none of them should be quoted as a project result.

## 3. The Chaitin archive correction

A correction is supported by the sources, and it comes from the relayed side. The ChatGPT relays of 24 November at 1:10 AM and 1:14 AM raise three points against the closing discussion of docs/archive/chaitin-lecture-notes.md, and the archive page as it stands in the mirror still carries the wording those points address, so the correction is owed and not yet landed on that page.

First, program size minima. The archive discussion speaks of searching programs in size order for the smallest one whose output reproduces a chosen encoding, and of recording the ratio between that program and the table it regenerates. For a universal machine, a found generator establishes an upper bound only. A search that has found no shorter program has not proved that none exists, because an unfinished shorter program may halt later. Exact minima can be certified inside a finite candidate set where every relevant execution is decidable or explicitly bounded in time, which is the special case the finite map of rows 17 and 18 actually occupies.

Second, lower bounds. The lecture summary itself reports the result that no lower bound on program size complexity can ever be proved. The relayed correction narrows that sentence: particular lower bounds can be provable, and the incompleteness result restricts proving sufficiently high complexity bounds inside a fixed suitable theory. The summary sentence as written is broader than the result it reports.

Third, the halting probability. The archive discussion reads the clean exit fraction of the fuzzing supervisors as an ever improving lower bound on an empirical halting probability. A sample fraction is not a lower bound on a probability, since one successful run gives a fraction of 1 whatever the underlying probability is, and a timeout, a crash, a clean exit and mathematical halting are distinct observations. A genuine monotone lower bound needs a fixed prefix free machine and a sum of the weights 2 to the power of minus the program length, taken once for each distinct program actually observed to halt. Universality is needed for the strong randomness properties of the resulting number, not for the weighted sum bound itself.

No other correction to the archive page is supported by the sources read for this consolidation. In particular, nothing in the sources supports changing the lecture summary sections on Hilbert, on the incompleteness results, on Turing, or on Omega as a defined number, beyond the narrowing in the second point above.
